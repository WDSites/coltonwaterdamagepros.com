Winter storm season and Rialto's drainage channels

Most storm-driven water calls in this part of the Inland Empire follow the winter storm season, when heavy rain runs off paved ground fast.

The National Weather Service summary for 2023 recorded atmospheric river storms in January and March, plus Tropical Storm Hilary in August, and those covered the mountains and foothills of the region rather than Rialto alone. The practical lesson is that one wet season can bring several separate storms, so a home dried after the first one can take on water again.

Rialto has real flood-control work behind it. San Bernardino County completed the Cactus Basins No.

4 and 5 project, announced in December 2023, to hold large storm runoff designed around a 100-year storm. A county environmental notice filed in January 2026 also describes improving the Rialto Channel, about 2.97 miles between Willow Avenue and Etiwanda Avenue, by replacing undersized culverts. Those projects reduce risk but do not remove it, so check the FEMA flood map for your address before you assume your street is dry.

San Bernardino County's own flood history page lists county-wide floods in 1938 and 1969, and it does not name Rialto in those entries. We therefore make no claim about a specific Rialto flood. What matters for a homeowner is simpler: rainwater that enters a house is usually treated as Category 2 or 3 water, and our flood damage restoration crews handle the extraction, removal of porous materials and drying that follow.

Warehouse roofs and sprinkler lines in the Las Colinas area

The north end of Rialto, including the Las Colinas area, is home to major distribution centers. Large flat roofs, rooftop drains and fire-sprinkler lines fail differently from a house plumbing line, and a single leak can wet a lot of floor area and pallet storage. Water still spreading in Rialto? Six things to do before a crew pulls up

Cut the supply

Close the main valve when a pipe, hose or heater let go. If the water is coming off a roof or through a door, step away from that entry point.

Respect the electrics

Flip breakers only when the panel is reachable on dry ground. Water around outlets or the panel means stay outside and phone the utility.

Raise the valuables

Put electronics, paperwork, shoes and small furniture up on counters or beds, and get rugs and pillows off soaked floors.

Skip dirty water

Toilet, drain or channel water carries contamination. Keep kids and pets away, and wear boots if you have to wade through.

Take photos first

Shoot wide and close pictures of the water line and the source before moving anything, since your insurer will want that record.

Ring dispatch

Have your cross streets, the source and the wet rooms ready for the call, and ask what to do while the crew is on its way.
