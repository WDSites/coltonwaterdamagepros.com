Where the Cooley Ranch name comes from

Cooley Ranch is named for George Cooley, who bought about 200 acres along the Santa Ana River in 1854. A public encyclopedia entry on Colton adds that the area was known as Indian Knolls for nearly 100 years, and that local people sheltered on its knolls during the great flood of 1862.

Today Cooley Ranch is a residential part of eastern Colton. Cooley Ranch Elementary School, at 1000 South Cooley Drive, serves kindergarten through sixth grade and opened in 1991, according to California school data. We do not use that date to guess how old your house is.

What the Colton General Plan says about the river

The Santa Ana River is Colton's main flood risk and its main flood control feature, according to the city's General Plan safety element. The document says some stretches of the river are not protected by certified levees, and that a failure of Seven Oaks Dam, about 12 miles upstream, could cause serious flooding on both banks.

The same plan says Colton takes part in the National Flood Insurance Program and that its storm drains are sized to handle a 100-year storm at minimum. It does not say which blocks of Cooley Ranch are affected. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified, so check your address with FEMA or the City.

Flood water, plumbing water and the policy that covers each

Know which kind of loss you have

Flood is not part of a standard homeowners policy, according to the state insurance department, and flood coverage is a separate policy with a 30 day wait before it starts. Water from a plumbing failure inside the house is usually handled under the homeowners policy, depending on its terms.

That split is why the first photos should show where the water came from. Do not assume anything is covered or excluded. Read the policy, call your insurer and keep every receipt and record we give you.

Outside water and inside leaks need different work

Rain or river water that enters from outside is treated as contaminated. It can carry soil, sewage and chemicals, which means removal of soaked porous materials and careful disinfection. Our flood damage restoration and storm water damage pages explain that process.

A clean supply line leak is a different job. If a toilet or drain line has backed up, see our sewage backup cleanup page. For soaked carpet, debris and odor in any category, water damage cleanup covers the removal and sanitizing stages.

Safety first when water has come from outside

Stay out of moving water

Do not wade or drive through floodwater around the property. Depth and current are hard to judge.

Treat it as unsafe electrically

If water reached outlets, appliances or the meter, keep everyone out of the room until power is confirmed off by a professional or the utility.

Assume contamination

Keep children and pets away. Household fans and ordinary vacuums are not suitable for sewage or floodwater.

Shut off the water only when safe

If a pipe is also leaking, close the main valve only when you can reach it without standing in water.

Photograph from the doorway

Record the waterline and damaged belongings before anything is moved.

What drying looks like after a river-side loss

After extraction, crews open wet wall bases if needed, set air movers and dehumidifiers and read moisture in framing and subfloor until it matches dry reference readings. See structural drying for the equipment and the daily checks.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard is one reference for these steps. We use it as a guide, not as a credential. Some belongings and some materials cannot be saved, and we will say so plainly.
