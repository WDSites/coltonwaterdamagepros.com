Grand Terrace neighborhoods between Blue Mountain and the La Loma Hills

Grand Terrace covers only 3.51 square miles, but it climbs. The city lies between Blue Mountain on the east and the La Loma Hills on the west, so many lots slope and storm runoff has a clear direction. A home below a slope can take water against a garage door or a back wall in one heavy storm.

Still-spreading water, step by step: what to do before a crew reaches your door

Turn off the water

Turn the main valve clockwise, or the fixture valve if you know it. Every minute the source runs adds to the drying time.

Mind the electricity

If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, stay clear. Switch power off for wet areas only if the breaker is dry and easy to reach.

Slope runoff stays outside

If water is coming off the hillside, do not stand in it to move sandbags. Call us and stay indoors.

Get things up high

Put cushions, books and electronics on higher furniture, and lift loose rugs so they stop soaking.

Keep everyone clear

Storm and sewage water can carry contamination. Keep children and pets away from anything standing.

The Terrace, the Gage Canal and the farm-era lots

The area was once called the Terrace, and by about 1898 it carried the name Grand Terrace. Settlers planted citrus, then switched to walnuts and peaches after the 1913 freeze, and the 22.5 mile Gage Canal carried irrigation water from the Santa Ana River marshlands to the Terrace. A lot that was farmed this way may still hold buried irrigation lines that no one has mapped, so tell us about unusual wet spots in a yard or slab.

The city incorporated on November 30, 1978, becoming the 16th city in San Bernardino County, after a vote of 82 percent. The Building and Safety counter at City Hall, 22795 Barton Road, is the place to ask whether a repair needs a permit.
