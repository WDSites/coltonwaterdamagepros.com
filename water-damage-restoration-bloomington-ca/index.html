Septic systems and sewage backups in Bloomington

Bloomington is an unincorporated community in San Bernardino County, and much of it still relies on septic systems instead of sewer lines. A county notice filed in January 2026 describes a state-funded septic-to-sewer planning study through County Service Area 70. That is planning, not a finished sewer network, so many properties still depend on their own tank and leach field.

This changes what a backup means. A drain that gurgles or a toilet that will not clear can point to a full or failing septic system, not only a clogged line, and the water that comes up is contaminated. Do not touch it, keep children and pets away, and call us. Our flood damage restoration crews handle sewage cleanup under the Category 3 water rules, and the tank itself is a job for a septic contractor.

Once the line is cleared, containment and sanitizing follow the steps on our sewage backup cleanup page, and a failed household supply line is handled as burst pipe water damage on the clean-water side.

When water is spreading in Bloomington

Water damage restoration in Bloomington, CA is a race against absorption. Water soaks into drywall, subfloor and insulation within hours, so the first call matters more than the first cleanup attempt. Dispatch picks up day and night, and a crew can be sent the same day if one is free.

Shut off the main valve for any supply line problem. If water is near outlets or the panel, do not step into it. Tell dispatch your cross streets, because recent county road work on streets such as Jurupa Avenue and Locust Avenue can change which route crews take.

West Valley Water District, county permits and who to call

West Valley Water District serves much of Bloomington, so check your bill to confirm your provider. The district serves roughly 100,000 customers across the area, with 23 wells and 417 miles of pipe, according to county community council minutes. If a main line breaks in the street, the district is the call. If the leak is inside your walls, we are.

Because Bloomington is unincorporated, permit questions go to San Bernardino County Land Use Services instead of a city office. Ask them before you rebuild walls or replace flooring. Our water damage repair crews can describe what was cut out and what needs replacing, which helps when you talk to the county.

The 1888 town grid and the Valley Boulevard corridor

The original Bloomington town site was laid out in 1888 by the Semi-Tropic Land and Water Company, the forerunner of West Valley Water District. It was bounded by Valley Boulevard on the north, Slover Avenue on the south, Larch Avenue on the east and Linden Avenue on the west. Many older homes and small businesses along that grid sit on aging plumbing.

The tie-back is simple. In a property that old, a supply line or a drain can fail slowly inside a wall, and a damp baseboard or a soft patch of floor is often the first sign. Ask for a moisture reading before you repaint or replace flooring.

Rail yard, quarry edge and commercial leaks

South Bloomington includes the old Crestmore quarry area, where the Riverside Portland Cement plant opened in 1907. The community also has the Union Pacific Alhambra Subdivision hump yard, and I-10 runs through it. Industrial and commercial buildings sit close to homes here.

For owners of those buildings, roof drains, flat roofs and fire lines are as likely a source as plumbing. Tell dispatch whether the space is a shop, warehouse or house. Our water mitigation crews map the wet area with moisture meters and thermal imaging before deciding what to dry in place.

Larger households and everyday supply-line leaks

The 2020 Census counted 5,925 housing units in Bloomington, about 97.9 percent occupied and 67.0 percent owner-occupied, with an average household size of 4.18. Bigger households run more laundry, more dishwashers and more showers every day. Washing machine hoses, toilet supply lines and under-sink valves take the most wear.

Replacing braided hoses on a schedule and knowing where your main shutoff is costs little. When one fails, a small leak becomes a wet room fast. If it happens on a weekend, the 24/7 line covers it.

Water still spreading in Bloomington? Start with these six steps

Close the valve

Shut the main for a burst line or failed hose. With a sewage backup, stop flushing and running every drain and toilet in the house.

Watch the panel

Kill power to wet areas only when the panel is dry and easy to reach. If water got into the panel itself, stay out and ring the utility.

Move belongings up

Get electronics, papers and small furniture onto dry surfaces, and lift cushions and rugs off the wet floor.

Leave tank water alone

Anything that rose from a drain or a septic tank is contaminated. Keep people and pets clear, and skip fans or a household vacuum.

Record the damage

Photograph the water line, the source and damaged items before you shift them.

Phone the line

Tell dispatch your cross streets, where the water started and which rooms are wet. A crew can come the same day when one is free.
