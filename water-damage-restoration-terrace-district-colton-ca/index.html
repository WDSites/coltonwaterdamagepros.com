The Terrace District in Colton

A 2020 historical resource report on California's CEQAnet database lists six historic districts in Colton. One of them is the Terrace District, described there as the city's first and largest citrus growing area. The report gives its boundaries as Mill Street on the north, Rancho Avenue on the west and Pennsylvania Avenue on the east, with J Street on the south.

The same report quotes an older city document that puts the southern edge at Interstate 10 instead. Because the sources differ, we do not draw a firm line on a map. If you want to know whether an address is inside a historic district, the City of Colton Planning Division offers an online property viewer for that.

Why history changes how water damage gets handled

Colton's historic preservation rules apply to designated properties and districts. The city's application form for a Certificate of Appropriateness covers new construction, additions, alterations, signs, accessory structures and demolition on historic properties.

So a repair that changes the outside of a building, such as replacing siding, windows or porch parts, may need review. We do not decide that for you. Ask the Planning Division before demolition, and keep damaged historic pieces until you know what the review requires.

FEMA flood-zone status for the Terrace District has not been verified. We do not call the district flood-prone, and a historic designation says nothing about flood risk.

Drying first, then deciding what to replace

The first goal is to stop damage from growing. Standing water is removed, wet materials are assessed with meters and drying equipment is placed to pull moisture from framing and wall cavities. See our structural drying page for the equipment used.

Where a historic feature is wet, such as baseboards, trim, plaster or wood flooring, we explain whether it can be dried and kept or needs replacement. That choice belongs to you, your insurer and, where it applies, the City. Our water damage repair page explains how rebuilding is scoped after drying.

Documenting a historic home

Photographs of original trim, flooring and fixtures before and after the loss help show what was there. If a piece must be removed, label it and store it dry. Do not discard it until the scope of repair is settled.

Insurance and historic features

A standard policy may treat historic materials differently from ordinary ones. Ask your insurer how it handles original materials and what records it wants. Coverage depends on the cause of the water and your policy terms, and nothing here predicts an outcome.

Safety steps in an older home

Shut off the supply if safe

Close the main valve if you can reach it without standing in water.

Be careful with old wiring

If water touched outlets, fixtures or a panel, stay out of the room and do not run electrical equipment in wet areas.

Do not disturb old finishes

Do not sand, scrape or tear into painted trim or plaster you suspect is old. Dust and debris can be a hazard.

Photograph and wait

Take photos and call before anything is pulled out.

Keep the water category in mind

If it came from a sewer line or outside, keep people and pets away and call.

Utilities, codes and next steps

The City of Colton supplies water, wastewater and electric service and runs its own Building and Safety Division, which applies the 2022 California Building Standards Codes. If your repair needs a permit, that office is the place to ask. Our emergency water removal page covers what happens first when water is standing.
