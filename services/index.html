Pick the service by what you see

Water damage work happens in stages, and each stage has its own crew, equipment and goal. The first stage stops the water and gets it out. The next dries the structure, removes what cannot be saved, and rebuilds. Mold work sits beside those stages because it only happens where moisture lingers.

If water is standing on the floor, start with emergency water removal. If the water is gone but walls, subfloors and cabinets are still wet, water mitigation is the drying stage that protects what remains. Once everything reads dry, water damage repair brings drywall, flooring and paint back.

How the services fit together

Most losses need more than one service in sequence. A burst pipe, for example, begins with extraction and source control, moves into drying, and ends with repair. Mold appears only when wet materials stay damp, which is why drying speed matters so much.

When contaminated water is involved, flood damage restoration covers the stricter protocols for river, storm and sewage water. Wet debris and ruined materials are handled by water damage cleanup, which includes sanitizing and deodorizing after the water is out.

Our secondary service, mold remediation, covers assessment, containment and removal of mold that follows a leak, with testing afterward. California does not license mold assessors or remediators, so ask any company how it plans to follow the S520 standard.

How pricing and scope are handled

One habit runs through every service here: you hear the scope and the price first. Contaminated or clean water, how many rooms are wet, which materials took the hit, how many days drying machines run and how much gets rebuilt all shape that number. Nobody can state it honestly before someone looks at the loss.

Insurance questions get general answers only. A sudden burst is often covered while a slow seep often is not, and rising floodwater typically falls under separate flood coverage. Your policy and your insurer hold the real answer. Call the line any hour and a coordinator will explain the next step.
