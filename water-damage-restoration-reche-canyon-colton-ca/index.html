Reche Canyon and the northern edge of Colton

Reche Canyon is a long canyon that runs north to south through the foothills at the edge of the San Bernardino Valley. A public encyclopedia entry for the canyon puts the canyon floor at about 1,043 feet and places the county line near its midpoint. The northern half is mostly inside the City of Colton, a small piece belongs to Loma Linda, and the southern half is unincorporated Riverside County.

This page covers the Colton side of the canyon. If you are not sure which side of the county line your address sits on, your property tax bill or the county assessor can tell you. That matters for permits, utilities and who handles your rebuild approvals.

What the city says about rain and flooding in the canyon

The City of Colton General Plan safety element says that flooding from rain in Colton is largely limited to the areas immediately next to the Santa Ana River and Reche Canyon. The same document notes that Reche Canyon lies in the Box Springs Mountains, where a 1981 wildfire burned more than 4,000 acres and four smaller wildfires have since touched parts of the canyon.

That is a citywide planning statement, not a map of your road. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. To check an address, use FEMA's Flood Map Service Center or ask the City's Building and Safety Division. A mapped zone does not tell you whether your house will or will not get wet.

We do not call any single road or lot flood-prone. Water also enters canyon homes through a roof, a door threshold, a window or a failed pipe in any kind of weather.

Everyday causes inside a canyon home

Most water losses start inside the house. A supply line lets go, a water heater tank seeps, a washing machine hose splits or a dishwasher leaks under the cabinets. Our pages on burst pipe cleanup, water heater leaks and appliance leaks explain how each one is handled.

Heavy rain adds a second group of problems, such as roof leaks, water under a door and saturated ground against a wall. Our storm water damage page covers that side of the work. Do not assume a stain is old because the weather has cleared.

Reaching a canyon address

Reche Canyon Road is a narrow two lane road. A 2026 report in the Inland Empire Community News quotes Colton's planning manager rating traffic on the road at level of service F near a proposed commercial plaza, and residents there describe a rural setting where people keep horses.

For a restoration job, that means equipment trucks plan the approach in advance. Tell us your road, any gate code, the driveway length and where a hose can run. We cannot promise an arrival time, and we will not quote one over the phone.

First steps and safety before help arrives

Stop the source if it is safe

Close the main valve or the valve under the fixture if you can reach it without stepping through water.

Keep clear of electricity

Stay out of standing water near outlets, cords and the panel. Do not flip breakers with wet hands or wet feet.

Move people and animals

Keep children, pets and horses away from wet floors and from any water that may carry sewage or runoff.

Photograph the damage

Take clear photos of wet areas and damaged items once the space is safe to enter.

Lift only what is dry and safe

Raise small items off a wet floor. Leave heavy furniture and anything near live power alone.

How we dry a canyon home

Pumps or extraction units take out any standing water first. After that, crews map moisture in walls and floors with meters, set air movers and dehumidifiers and take readings again each visit. Our structural drying and water damage cleanup pages show what each stage involves.

The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the reference for drying decisions. That is a reference, not a claim about anyone's certification. Not every material can be saved. Drywall or insulation that took in dirty water is usually removed.

Insurance and your records

Photos, readings and a written scope

Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope can help describe the loss. Whether any of it is covered depends on the cause and the terms of your policy, so read the policy and speak to your insurer before you agree to anything.

Under California Department of Insurance guidance, flood needs its own policy and is separate from the standard homeowners policy. Water from a burst pipe or appliance is treated differently from rising ground water.

Mold and moisture afterward

Mold needs moisture, so a wall that stays damp is the real concern. No California license exists for mold assessors or remediators, which is why a written scope matters. Our mold remediation page explains containment and testing. Neighbors in Loma Linda share part of the northern canyon.
