A community with its own history inside Colton

Cal State San Bernardino's South Colton Oral History Project describes South Colton as a community of about 1.3 square miles inside Colton's city limits. It says the neighborhood was largely self-sustaining before 1960 and that it remains a predominantly Mexican heritage community.

The project's photographs include Our Lady of the Holy Rosary church and Wilson Junior High. Those are landmarks of a lived-in residential area, and they are the kind of places neighbors use to describe where they live.

What shapes water damage here

We do not assume that every South Colton house is old or that old houses leak more. The sources do not give a construction date for the neighborhood's homes. What we do know is that a house of any age can lose a supply line, a drain connection or a water heater, and that the first hours matter more than the year it was built.

If you are unsure what is leaking, our burst pipe cleanup, appliance leak and water heater leak pages describe the signs of each. A kitchen or bathroom floor that is damp for no clear reason is worth a call.

FEMA flood-zone status for South Colton has not been verified, and we do not describe the area as flood-prone. Most calls here start with a failure inside the house, not a flood.

Water, sewer and who to call

The City of Colton draws its drinking water from deep wells and runs its own wastewater collection system, according to the city's utilities pages. The same department handles billing for all three services.

When a toilet or floor drain backs up, the blockage can be in your private line or in the city main. The utility can help sort that out. Do not use fans or an ordinary vacuum on sewage. See sewage backup cleanup for how that work is handled.

Slover Mountain and the south side of the city

South Colton sits near Slover Mountain. County historians note that limestone quarrying on the hill began in 1881 and that a cement plant opened there in 1894. Decades of mining reduced the mountain to a small hill.

It is a reminder that this side of the city grew around rail and industry. The City's cultural resources page notes that the Southern Pacific reached Colton in 1875 and that the community grew around the railroad. None of this changes how we dry a wet floor, but it explains why street patterns and lots here look the way they do.

Safety steps while you wait for a call back

Find the valve

Close the main shut-off, usually near the meter or on an outside wall, if you can reach it dry.

Respect the wiring

Stay out of any room where water is near outlets, ceiling fixtures or the panel.

Pull out what is easy to save

Lift small rugs, shoes and electronics off wet floors only if the room is safe.

Photograph before you clean

Show the source, the wet areas and anything damaged.

Leave sewage alone

If the water is gray or smells like waste, keep people and pets out and call.

Drying, repair and your records

A restoration visit usually starts with a moisture map and extraction, followed by drying equipment and daily readings. Our emergency water removal and structural drying pages describe the stages. Repairs such as new drywall and paint follow only after readings show the structure is dry.

Insurance and paperwork

Photos and a written scope can help document the loss. Coverage depends on the cause and your policy, so ask your insurer before work that you want considered for a claim.
