What a slab leak is and where it starts

A slab leak is a pressurized water line that fails inside or beneath the concrete floor of a house. Many single-story homes across Southern California sit on slabs, so supply and hot-water pipes often run in or under the concrete. No public source tracks slab leaks by city, so we will not quote you a Colton rate.

Plumbers name several causes for a failed under-slab line: pinhole corrosion in copper, abrasion where a pipe rubs concrete, a bad joint, and ground movement that stresses a fitting. Which one applies to your house is a question for the plumber who locates the break. Our work begins once water has reached the floor, the walls or the subfloor.

South Colton is the older part of town. The San Bernardino County history page on Slover Mountain says limestone quarrying began there in 1881 and the first cement plant west of the Rockies opened in 1894. Houses and small shops from those decades and after carry older plumbing, and age alone does not predict a leak, but older lines have had more years to wear.

When a slab leak is an emergency

A slab leak becomes an emergency when water comes up through the floor, pools at a wall base or soaks flooring in more than one room. Someone answers the phone all night and all weekend, and once a crew is free it heads out that day.

A slow slab leak behaves differently. The slab absorbs water for days or weeks, flooring loosens a little at a time, and the first hint can be a utility bill. Even a slow leak deserves a prompt call, because a wet slab keeps feeding the walls above it.

First-hour safety steps for a slab leak

Handle the water first and the power second. These steps apply to clean supply water only. Sewage or floodwater calls for the separate precautions on our sewage backup cleanup page.

  1. Find the main shutoff valve and close it. On its conservation page, City of Colton Public Utilities asks customers to learn where the private shut-off valve sits. Find yours now, not mid-leak.
  2. Water at an outlet, an appliance or the breaker panel means keeping out of that area and not touching switches. Ring the City of Colton electric utility or 911, then reach us.
  3. A gas odor means walking out of the house and phoning SoCalGas or 911 from the yard.
  4. Flip the breakers serving wet rooms only if the panel stands on dry ground that you can get to without wading.
  5. Lift rugs, shoes, electronics and cardboard onto dry surfaces, and photograph every wet area plus the meter reading.
  6. Call a licensed plumber for leak detection, then call us for drying and removal.

Hot water and live wiring

A leaking hot-water line can scald, and slab water can reach wiring in walls and floor outlets. Treat any wet outlet, warm puddle or crackling sound as an electrical hazard and leave the area.

Who finds the leak and what our crew does

Finding a leak under concrete is plumbing work. A plumber uses electronic listening gear, line pressure tests and sometimes thermal imaging to locate the break, and may route a new line through the attic or walls instead of cutting the slab. We do not replace pipe.

Our work is the water the leak left behind. A crew maps moisture with pin and pinless meters and a thermal imaging camera, pulls baseboards, and cuts small relief openings where water has wicked up a wall. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers then dry the cavities and the room.

If the plumber opens the slab, expect concrete dust and a wet trench. We schedule drying around that work so the same room is not soaked twice. Equipment detail lives on our structural drying page, and a line that bursts inside a wall is covered under burst pipe water damage.

Flooring, baseboards and vapor from a wet slab

Concrete holds water like a sponge and releases it slowly as vapor. That is why flooring over a leaking slab can cup, bubble or lift weeks after the pipe is fixed. Tile may stay put while the grout and the thinset beneath it hold moisture.

What can be saved depends on material and time. Laminate and engineered wood swell at the edges and often go. Carpet and pad can be pulled and dried when the water is clean and the pad is lifted early, as described on our carpet water damage page. Solid hardwood needs readings before anyone decides.

We follow drying goals from ANSI/IICRC S500, the published reference for water damage restoration, and record readings at each visit. A slab that stays damp sends moisture into drywall bottoms and cabinet bases, and mold needs moisture, so drying the wall base matters as much as removing the visible puddle.

What changes the cost of a slab leak job

Slab leak jobs vary more than most water losses because the hidden path of the water is wider than the wet spot suggests.

How far the water traveled

One room and a hallway is a smaller job than a whole slab with water behind every exterior wall.

Flooring type

Tile, laminate, carpet and hardwood dry or fail differently, so the removal scope follows the floor.

Whether the slab is opened

A plumber cutting concrete adds dust control, wet debris and a second round of drying.

Hot or cold line

A hot-water leak adds humidity and warms the slab, which changes how drying is planned.

Time since the leak began

A leak found on day one is smaller than one that soaked the slab for weeks and reached the walls.

Scope and price are laid out before any work begins. A call to dispatch gets you a plain account of what is pushing your total up or down, with no blind guess.

Slab leaks and your policy

Sudden failure versus long seepage

Insurance often treats a sudden pipe failure differently from a leak that seeped for months. Many policies cover the resulting water damage when a line fails suddenly, and many exclude damage from long-term seepage. Finding and repairing the pipe itself is often handled separately from the damage it caused.

Plumbing water is not flood. The state's consumer claims guide says homeowners policies leave flood out but usually pay for other water damage. Read your policy and call your insurer before major repairs. We document with photos and moisture readings so the file shows what the water touched.

Mistakes to avoid and ways to catch a leak sooner

  • Waiting to see whether a warm floor spot fades. A hot-water leak under the slab only grows.
  • Running a space heater or household vacuum over a wet floor.
  • Laying new flooring or painting over damp material before it is dried and read.
  • Ignoring a water bill that jumps. The City of Colton Public Utilities conservation page advises customers to check their meter and bill to track usage, and a sudden jump is the easiest early warning.
  • Skipping the plumber because the surface looks dry. Leak detection finds the cause, and drying alone will not stop the pipe.
