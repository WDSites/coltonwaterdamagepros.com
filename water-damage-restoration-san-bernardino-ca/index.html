When water is spreading in San Bernardino

Water does not wait for business hours, and in a city of more than 222,000 people it never lacks a place to go. A failed supply line, a leaking roof or a backed-up drain can soak drywall and subfloor in a few hours. Our emergency dispatch is available around the clock for San Bernardino, and the first call is about stopping the damage.

While you wait for a crew, shut off the main water valve if the leak is plumbing, keep people out of the wet room, and lift small valuables off the floor. If the water is near outlets or a panel, stay back and call the utility or 911 about the electrical risk first. Then call us, because the drying clock starts when the water arrives.

Water still spreading in San Bernardino? Do these six things while the crew is en route

Find the valve

Close the shut-off under the fixture, or the main if the fixture valve is out of reach. A leak that keeps running undoes any extraction we do.

Check power before you step in

If water reaches outlets, a panel or a garage door opener, do not wade in. Cut power at the panel only if you can reach it dry.

Get belongings off the floor

Set electronics, paper files and small furniture on counters or beds, and drag rugs off wet floors so they stop wicking.

Keep people and pets out

Standing water from a toilet, floor drain or outside flooding may carry contamination. Keep children and animals in a dry room.

Photograph before you clean

Take wide and close shots of the damage and the source. Insurers ask for them, and they cost nothing to take.

The water problem San Bernardino has most: runoff from the north and the creek channels

The Santa Ana River forms part of the city's southern border, and City Creek, Lytle Creek, Twin Creek, San Timoteo Creek and Warm Creek feed it through channels modified for flood control. San Bernardino County's Department of Public Works records the 1862 flood as the largest on record, and flooding in 1916, 1938, 1969 and 1998 followed. A property near a channel or at the foot of a canyon therefore deserves a hard look after any heavy storm.

The National Weather Service noted that the January 2023 storms sent runoff through normally dry channels in San Bernardino. If runoff or groundwater has entered your home, the job is flood damage restoration, and the water is treated as contaminated until proven otherwise. Check the FEMA flood map for your address to see where you sit.

Older core, newer edges, and a large renter share

Census data puts owner-occupancy in San Bernardino at 50.0 percent, with about 3.36 people per household. That means half the homes are rentals, and a leak under a tenant's sink often travels through a shared wall or ceiling to a neighbor. We document each unit so a landlord and a tenant can sort out who covers what.

Washer hoses, toilet seals and supply lines wear out in any home, and in a rental the repair often waits on a landlord. If you rent, tell your landlord in writing the same day, then call us so emergency water removal can start before the carpet pad sours.

Local landmarks and what they tell us about water damage

Downtown holds the Santa Fe Depot, the California Theatre and the San Bernardino County Courthouse, which sit among older commercial blocks. Older buildings often have long runs of aging supply and drain pipe, so a failure in an upper unit can soak several floors below it. In a mixed-use block we map moisture floor by floor before deciding what to dry.

Around Seccombe Lake at Sierra Way and 5th Street and in the University District near California State University San Bernardino, the housing is a mix of single family homes and apartments. Student and rental use means a leak may sit unreported over a weekend, so we check moisture in the ceilings below, not just the room where you saw the stain.

In the Perris Hill and Shandin Hills areas, sloped lots send storm runoff toward garages and low rooms. Up at Arrowhead Springs, about 3,000 feet up and within the San Bernardino National Forest, heavy rain can bring debris flow and fast water against foundations. Hillside owners should keep drains clear before the winter storm season.

What drives the cost of a San Bernardino water loss

Water category

Clean supply water costs less to handle than gray water, and sewage or flood water needs protective gear and disposal of porous materials.

Affected area

The number of rooms and floors the water reached sets how much equipment and how many technicians are needed.

Materials involved

Carpet pad, drywall, insulation and hardwood respond differently, and some can be dried while others must come out.

Equipment and run time

Air movers and dehumidifiers stay on site for days, and the count depends on how wet the structure got and how fast work began.

Rebuild scope

Repair of walls, flooring and paint is quoted separately once drying readings are normal.

Insurance works differently for different kinds of loss in a city this size. A burst line or sudden leak is often a covered event under a homeowners policy, while a slow drip or deferred maintenance often is not. Rising water from outside typically falls under a separate flood policy. The policy language and a call to your insurer settle the question, and call us for a figure on your own loss, with the scope laid out before work begins.
